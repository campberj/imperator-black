# PoC Opposition Force: Freespace Solutions

**Status:** Accepted (2026-10-20).

## Identity

**Freespace Solutions** is a privatized debt and salvage corporation. On paper it repossesses ships and stations from defaulting owners and salvages wrecks. Off the books, it conducts **grey-zone operations for paying patrons**: seizing ships, holding crews, moving cargo and erasing evidence, all under the cover of a lawful debt claim.

Its **current patron is the Meridian Directorate**, a technocratic state run through its intelligence ministries. The Directorate pays for deniable action, supplies targets and intelligence, and protects Freespace from prosecution, while publicly denying any connection.

Freespace has three layers, and each shows up on the table.

| Layer | What it is | What it gives Freespace | How it appears in play |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Patron** | Whoever is paying. Currently the Meridian Directorate. | Targets, intelligence, legal cover, the odd piece of state-grade hardware | Rare Directorate handlers and deniable operators at the top threat tier; documents and prisoners that point back to the Directorate |
| **Corporate core** | Freespace's own crews and security, run like a business with criminal underpinnings: repossession by force, debt enforcement, intimidation | Ships, stations, crews, cash, local muscle | Most enemies on the table: salvage crews, asset security, collections enforcers, liquidators, techs |
| **Contractors** | Mercenary companies and hired guns paid by the job | Professional boarding and reaction capability Freespace doesn't train itself | The reaction force at high alert, and attached specialists |

**Campaign thread: attribution.** Intelligence recovered from Freespace operations (data cores, prisoners, a handler's body) builds evidence tying Freespace to the Meridian Directorate. Exposing the link can escalate the campaign into a deniable war with the Directorate, where the peer naval-infantry opponent enters. Because Freespace works for whoever pays, a later campaign can give it a different patron.

**Mission hook: the debt claim.** Many Freespace seizures are technically legal. Retaking a "repossessed" ship may mean fighting people with paperwork on their side, which suits campaigns where command guidance is tight and rules of engagement matter.

## Mechanics adopted for playtesting

### Contact tokens

Numbered poker chips start as **unknown contacts**.

- A contact is revealed when a player model gains line of sight to it, or when noise occurs within a set distance.
- On reveal, roll on the mission's **contact table**. The app prints it, tuned to the threat tier. Results run from *false alarm* through the tier's elements to *booby trap*.
- Unrevealed contacts move toward noise once the alert level allows it.

### Alert track (0–6)

| Raises alert | |
|---|---|
| Shooting (not suppressed or silenced) | +1 |
| Breaching a door or bulkhead | +1 |
| Explosion | +2 |
| A revealed enemy leaves your line of sight alive, or asset security uses Raise Alarm | +1 |

| Threshold | Effect |
|---|---|
| 2 | Unrevealed contacts start moving toward the last noise |
| 4 | The contractor reaction force arrives at a set entry point over the next rounds |
| 6 | Lockdown: a mission-specific event (doors seal, hostages are moved, data is wiped, the ship tries to break dock) |

### Surrender and prisoners

Cowed models who surrender become **prisoners**. They are a campaign resource (intelligence toward attribution, leverage, reputation) and a tactical burden (they need a guard to move).
