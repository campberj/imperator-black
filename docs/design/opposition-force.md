# PoC Opposition Force: The Corsairs

**Status:** Accepted (2026-10-20). Faction name still open; "the corsairs" is the working term.

## Identity

The corsairs are a **grey faction**: neither a state military nor a simple gang. They are built in three layers, and each layer shows up on the table.

| Layer | What it is | What it gives the corsairs | How it appears in play |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Patron** | A state-level power that sponsors them quietly: sealed letters of marque, intelligence handlers, occasional state-grade hardware. It publicly denies any connection. | Targets, intelligence, protection from prosecution, the odd piece of military kit | Rare elite handlers and deniable state operators at the top threat tier; documents and prisoners that point back to the patron |
| **Criminal core** | Smuggling, salvage, extortion and ship-seizure syndicates that own the crews and the money | Ships, stations, crews, cash, local muscle | Most of the enemies on the table: crew, guards, enforcers, cleaners, specialists |
| **Contractors** | Mercenary companies and individual hired guns paid by the job | Professional boarding and reaction capability the syndicates can't train themselves | The reaction force at high alert, and attached specialists |

**Why this matters to the campaign:** the patron is the long thread. Intelligence recovered from corsair ships (data cores, prisoners, a handler's body) builds toward **attributing** the corsairs to their patron. That can escalate the campaign into a deniable war with a state, where the peer naval-infantry opponent comes in.

The patron state and the faction's name are open setting decisions.

## Threat tiers

The mission generator builds the enemy force from tiers.

| Tier | Draws from |
|---|---|
| 1 — Low | Deck crew, guards |
| 2 — Medium | Tier 1 plus enforcers, specialists, automated defences |
| 3 — High | Tier 2 plus close-quarters enforcers, cleaners, hired guns; contractor reaction force at high alert |
| 4 — Story | Tier 3 plus patron handlers or deniable operators |

## Mechanics adopted for playtesting

### Contact tokens

Numbered poker chips start as **unknown contacts**.

- A contact is revealed when a player model gains line of sight to it, or when noise occurs within a set distance.
- On reveal, roll on the mission's **contact table**. The app prints it, tuned to the threat tier. Results run from *false alarm* through the tier's elements to *booby trap*.
- Unrevealed contacts move toward noise once the alert level allows it.

### Alert track (0–6)

| Raises alert | |
|---|---|
| Shooting (not suppressed or silenced) | +1 |
| Breaching a door or bulkhead | +1 |
| Explosion | +2 |
| A revealed enemy leaves your line of sight alive, or a guard uses Raise Alarm | +1 |

| Threshold | Effect |
|---|---|
| 2 | Unrevealed contacts start moving toward the last noise |
| 4 | The contractor reaction force arrives at a set entry point over the next rounds |
| 6 | Lockdown: a mission-specific event (doors seal, hostages are moved, data is wiped, the ship tries to break dock) |

### Surrender and prisoners

Cowed crew who surrender become **prisoners**. They are a campaign resource (intelligence toward attribution, ransom, reputation) and a tactical burden (they need a guard to move).
