# Air and void layer: notes and inspiration

Status: **notes only, no rules yet** (RJ, 2026-10-22). Drones, munitions, air defence and void craft wait for their own phase, after the proof of concept. This page gathers ideas so that phase starts from something.

The main source is Echelon Software's *Near Present* (2025), whose infantry and combat robots are now statted in chapter 9 (decision 9.10). Its rules are summarized here in our own words, as design reference, not as rules to copy.

## How Near Present handles the air

- **The airspace is a separate zone** beside the table, not a place on it. Each turn opens with an *airspace phase*, before any ground activations.
- **A budget, rolled each turn.** The attacker holds a reserve of munitions, priced in points. Each turn, a roll sets how many points of munitions can enter the airspace that turn, so firepower arrives unevenly.
- **Defences sit off the table.** The defender buys *emplacements*: short-range air defence with an ammunition die, counter-rocket guns, a sensor mast that sharpens all interceptions, and a vertical launcher holding the defender's own munitions. Munitions can strike the emplacements; ground troops can't reach them.
- **Strikes need eyes.** A munition can only strike inside the target area when something guides it in: a surveillance drone in the airspace, or a ground model with a spotting ability (the *Pusher*). Without guidance, munitions can only fight each other and the emplacements.
- **Interception and reaction.** When a munition activates, the other side may try to intercept with emplacements or air-to-air drones. Ground models standing outside, or at a door or window, may also fire at incoming drones, at a penalty.
- **A finite magazine.** In the campaign, munitions spent are gone for good. Each side draws from a fixed reserve across several linked battles, and a *stand-off strike* can be launched against an enemy base's defences, with no ground fight.

### The munitions

| Munition | What it does | Imperator Black analogue |
|---|---|---|
| Strike missile | Brings down a whole structure: everything inside tests, and the building becomes rubble and open ground | Ship-to-ship strike, orbital or naval fire mission |
| Flux (electromagnetic) missile | Shuts an emplacement down for a turn rather than destroying it | Ion or EMP strike on point defence |
| Surveillance (ISR) drone | Lets the other munitions strike targets inside the area | Ship's sensors, a recon drone, a spotter satellite |
| Air-to-air drone | Intercepts other munitions | Point-defence drone, interceptor |
| FPV drone | Strikes one model, door, window or wall section | Boarding drone, breaching drone |
| Loitering munition | A 3" blast on a point, model, wall or emplacement | Mortar-type fire mission, loitering drone |

### Ground units that touch the air

- **Systems Operator** and **Team Leader** spot for munitions (*Pusher*); the Systems Operator can also redirect incoming munitions with a cognitive jammer.
- **Grenadiers** can fire a flux grenade at a drone overhead.
- **Robots (CCT)** can carry launch tubes with three drones, and an ablative C-RAM module that fires at incoming drones.
- **Heavy-lift helicopters** deliver a team and its robots, with more slung underneath.

## Fit with Imperator Black

1. **It belongs at the end of the round, beside the mission clock.** The airspace could be one more step, run after the clock roll and before the alert effects. It would add a few dice and a decision each round, but no extra activations.
2. **Noise and the alert track already fit.** Any strike is Very loud. Calling one in could raise the alert, and a defender's sensor mast could stand for an alert-track bonus.
3. **Guidance gives specialists a job.** Requiring a spotter (a drone, or a model with a spotting skill) to strike inside the area keeps air power tied to the models on the table: kill the Systems Operator and the strikes stop.
4. **Void works the same way.** Point-defence guns stand in for the air-defence emplacements. Boarding torpedoes, strike craft and ship's guns stand in for the munitions. The ship's sensor suite is the surveillance drone, and the alert track becomes the target ship's battle stations.
5. **The finite magazine fits the campaign.** Munitions could be requisitioned in the ship and outpost layer (Phase 7 and after) and stay spent until restocked, the way Near Present's campaign reserve works.
6. **Keep it out of chapter 3's door sequence.** In Near Present, drones may breach doors from the air. Ours should only open the outside doors of buildings and hulls, so the boarding fight stays a boarding fight.

## Open questions

- Does the airspace step belong to every mission, or only those that name it (an air-support asset, a planetside raid, a ship-to-ship action)?
- One budget roll per round (Near Present's way) or a fixed number of strike points per mission (simpler to plan, and it fits the campaign layer)?
- Do drones become models on the table (with Size 1, already in the attribute list) or stay abstract in the airspace?
- How does a strike resolve against our wound table: a 2d10 or 2d12 blast with AP, like the existing grenades and self-destruct?
