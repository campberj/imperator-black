# Damage dice: matched pairs, half-steps and flat bonuses

Probability of each wound-table result, given a hit, before criticals. Armour 2 steps the row down by 2. Generated for decision 6.14 (half-step damage dice).

| Damage | Mean | Wounded or worse, Armour 0 | Down or worse, Armour 0 | Dead, Armour 0 | Down or worse, Armour 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2d4 | 5 | 38% | 0% | 0% | 0% |
| 2d6 | 7 | 72% | 17% | 0% | 0% |
| **d6+d8** | 8 | 79% | 31% | 0% | 2% |
| 2d8 | 9 | 84% | 44% | 2% | 9% |
| 2d6+2 (flat bonus) | 9 | 92% | 42% | 0% | 3% |
| d4+d12 (lopsided) | 9 | 79% | 46% | 2% | 12% |
| **d8+d10** | 10 | 88% | 55% | 8% | 19% |
| 2d8+1 (flat bonus) | 10 | 91% | 56% | 5% | 16% |
| 2d10 | 11 | 90% | 64% | 15% | 28% |
| **d10+d12** | 12 | 92% | 70% | 23% | 38% |
| 2d12 | 13 | 93% | 75% | 31% | 46% |

**Findings.**

- The half-steps (bold) sit halfway between the pairs on either side, giving eight evenly spaced steps instead of five.
- The same mean can play very differently: 2d6+2 almost always wounds but never kills outright and barely hurts armour; d4+d12 is erratic and the best of the three against armour; 2d8 sits between.
- Flat bonuses do little against armour, because armour removes whole rows and only the top of the roll matters. AP stays the anti-armour tool.
- Adopted: the three half-steps, used where a weapon sits between two steps. Not adopted: flat +N damage on weapons.
