# 16. Two players

*Draft. Everything in this chapter is Proposed until two-player games have been played.*

Imperator Black is built for solo play, but the rules underneath are the same whoever controls each side. This chapter replaces the solo opponent with a second player. It matches the forces by Troop Value, gives six missions in two families, and sets how much each side can see by mission.

- **Even fights:** both sides want the same thing, a meeting engagement, a fight over ground, or a race for salvage.
- **Raids:** one side attacks, one defends, using the four mission types of chapter 13. The attacker has initiative, stealth and the clock; the defender has the ground and a reserve that the attacker's own noise brings in.

More than two players split into two sides; each player runs their own models, and a side activates one model at a time as usual.

## What changes from the solo rules

<!-- table:versus.changes -->
- The solo opponent (chapter 10) is not used: no behavior cards, awareness states, marker, drill or surrender tests. The alarm and the alert track work only as this chapter says.
- "Player model" and "enemy model" in other chapters mean a model of the side named and a model of the other side.
- Initiative, actions, reactions, shooting, close combat, morale and casualties are unchanged. The mission clock is rolled once per round, by the side that won initiative.
<!-- /table -->

## Forces

<!-- table:versus.sizes -->
| Size | Troop Value | Models | Board | Mission clock |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Patrol** | 350 | About 5 to 8 a side | A 36" × 36" land table, or a deck (chapter 11) | 20 minutes, d4 |
| **Strike force** | 600 | About 9 to 14 a side | A 48" × 48" land table | 30 minutes, d6 |
<!-- /table -->

<!-- table:versus.forces -->
- Agree the game size. Each side builds a force of fireteams (standard fireteams, or ones built to their type: chapter 9) and characters, with a total Troop Value no higher than the size's limit (chapter 15).
- A fireteam may leave slots empty, but must field at least half of them, and its Leader if it has one. Each character joins a fireteam (at most one each) or acts alone.
- Choose loadouts from each troop's options. Use the troop's listed TV whatever its loadout; players who want exact values can work out the chosen loadout with chapter 15.
- Custom troops (chapter 15) only if both players agree.
- Either player may field any faction, including the same one; Automated models count as normal models for everything here.
<!-- /table -->

A Task Force Boone boarding element (a VAT fireteam and a Pathfinder) is 345 TV: a patrol. A generic line squad with its support team is 329.

## Choosing a mission

Agree a mission, or roll. Three are even fights and three are raids; how much each side can hide depends on the mission.

<!-- table:versus.mission_roll -->
| d6 | Mission | Kind | Hidden information |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Meeting engagement** | Even fight | Both sides deploy in blinds. |
| 2 | **Hold the ground** | Even fight | No blinds: both forces deploy openly. |
| 3 | **Salvage race** | Even fight | Both sides deploy in blinds. |
| 4 | **Raid: seize data or cargo** | Raid | The defender's garrison deploys in blinds. |
| 5 | **Raid: sabotage** | Raid | The defender's garrison deploys in blinds. |
| 6 | **Raid: recover or capture** | Raid | The defender's garrison deploys in blinds, and the mission's secret (below) is written down before deployment. |
<!-- /table -->

Build the board with chapter 11 (a deck) or chapter 12 (a land table). Roll the conditions or the hazard as normal.

## Even fights

<!-- table:versus.even -->
### Meeting engagement

*Two forces run into each other. Break them.*

- **Hidden information:** Both sides deploy in blinds.
- **Deployment:** Roll for edges; each side deploys within 6" of its own. On a deck, one side enters at the airlock and the other at the far end of the spine.
- **Scoring:** Casualty points only.

### Hold the ground

*Three points matter. Hold them.*

- **Hidden information:** No blinds: both forces deploy openly.
- **Deployment:** As a meeting engagement. Then place three objective markers: the middle of the table and, on the centre line, 12" to each side (on a deck: the heart and the two compartments nearest the middle of the spine).
- **Scoring:** From round 2, at the end of each round, each objective a side controls scores it 1 point. A side controls an objective with a standing model within 2" of it and no standing enemy model within 2".

### Salvage race

*Something valuable lies between you. Carry it home.*

- **Hidden information:** Both sides deploy in blinds.
- **Deployment:** As a meeting engagement. Then place three cache markers on the centre line: the middle, and 9" to each side (on a deck: the three compartments nearest the middle of the spine).
- **Scoring:** A model in base contact takes a cache with an Interact (as cargo, chapter 13: the carrier has −2" Move; a model in base contact takes it from a carrier that goes Down). A cache carried off a side's own edge (or out by its own entry on a deck) scores that side 3 points.
<!-- /table -->

## Raids

A raid is a chapter 13 mission with a player in the enemy's place. The defender's force wakes up the way the solo opponent's does: slowly, until the attacker makes noise or is seen.

### Setting up

<!-- table:versus.raid_setup -->
- The players roll off; the higher chooses to attack or defend. Build the board as chapter 13 does for the mission type: the objective or objectives, the attacker's entry and exit (the airlock or its own edge), and the defender's entry (the far end of the spine, or the far edge).
- The defender splits its force: a **guard**, at most a quarter of its force's TV, placed openly within 3" of the objective (of the first, for sabotage); a **reserve**, at least a quarter, off the board; and the **garrison**, the rest, in blinds.
- The defender places the garrison's blinds and its decoys anywhere more than 12" from the attacker's entry (aboard ship, not in the docking passage or a tile touching it). Then the attacker deploys as chapter 13 says.
- The mission clock is the attacker's: the size sets its die and time limit (chapter 13). The attacker activates first in round 1.
<!-- /table -->

### Secrets

Recover and capture raids hide one more thing. Write it down before deployment, and show it when it is revealed or at the end of the game.

<!-- table:versus.raid_secrets -->
- Recover: the defender marks three possible holding points, at least 12" apart and in the half of the board farther from the attacker's entry, and writes down which one holds the hostage. The hostage is revealed when an attacker model ends a Move within 3" of the right point, or sees it from within 6".
- Capture: the defender writes down which of its models is the high-value target (any troop with the Leader skill, or else any model of the guard). The target is revealed when it is Down, captured, or when an attacker model in base contact with it Interacts to identify it.
<!-- /table -->

### The alarm

<!-- table:versus.raid_alarm -->
- Until the alarm is raised, the garrison and guard are **unready**: an unready model may not Shoot, Throw, take Overwatch or react, and may Move only half its Move. Blinds do not move.
- The alarm is raised at the end of an activation in which a defender model sees an attacker model, is hit, or hears Loud noise (12" aboard ship, 24" on land) or any Very loud noise (chapter 10). From then on, no defender model is unready.
- A defender model that sees an attacker model may still act on it in that activation: the restriction lifts the moment it sees one.
<!-- /table -->

### The alert track

<!-- table:versus.raid_alert -->
- The alert track runs as in chapter 13: Loud noise +1, Very loud +2, the alarm +1, each check-in +1.
- At **2**: the garrison's blinds may move, up to 6" each activation.
- At **4**: the reserve arrives at the defender's entry at the end of the round (it may deploy in blinds).
- At **6**: the mission type's lockdown happens at the end of the round, carried out by the defender: the defender moves the hostage, runs the high-value target for its exit, or places a new blind within 3" of each standing sabotage target (a reserve team still off the board, or a decoy if none is left).
<!-- /table -->

### Grades

<!-- table:versus.raid_grades -->
- The attacker is graded with the mission type's table (chapter 13). The defender's grade is the mirror: attacker Decisive is defender Failure, Marginal is Marginal, and attacker Failure is defender Decisive.
<!-- /table -->

## Blinds

<!-- table:versus.blinds -->
1. A **blind** is a numbered chip standing for one team, or up to four models of a team (note which on paper). A **decoy** is a blind standing for nothing. Each side in a blinds mission adds two decoys; a raid defender adds three.
2. A blind activates as one activation: it Moves up to 6" (difficult ground and obstacles as normal) and may do nothing else. It cannot be shot, blocks nothing, and doesn't react.
3. A blind is **revealed** when an enemy model can see it, when it ends a Move within 3" of an enemy model, or at the start of any of its side's activations, if its player chooses. A blind that would cross an enemy model's line of sight during a Move stops and is revealed there.
4. On reveal, place its models with the first on the chip and the rest within 2" of it, in cover where possible, then remove the chip. A decoy is simply removed. Models revealed during the enemy's activation cannot react until that activation ends.
5. A blind revealed at the start of its own side's activation: place its models, then activate one of them as that activation.
<!-- /table -->

Blinds keep the enemy guessing: a chip on the far side of a hab block might be a fireteam on Overwatch or nothing at all. Note on paper which team each blind holds before deployment, and keep the paper face down.

## Ending the game and winning

<!-- table:versus.end -->
- The game ends when the mission clock reaches its time limit (the size sets the die and limit), when one side has no standing models on the board, or at the end of a round in which one side withdraws.
- **Withdrawal:** at the end of a round, a side with more than half of its starting models Down, Dead, captured or fled (not counting a reserve still off the board) withdraws; the game ends. In an even fight, the withdrawing side scores no mission points that round.
- Prisoners and surrender: either side may capture a Down, Bleeding or Critical enemy model in base contact with an Interact (chapter 7). Surrender tests (chapter 10) are not used: both sides' models fight on until broken, Down or captured.
<!-- /table -->

<!-- table:versus.casualty_points -->
- Each enemy model Down, Dead, captured or fled at the end of the game: 1 point. A Leader: 2. A model with Wounds 2 or more: 2 (a Leader with Wounds 2 or more: 3).
<!-- /table -->

**Even fights** are won on points:

<!-- table:versus.even_grades -->
- Add each side's mission points and casualty points. The side with more wins: **Decisive** if it has at least twice the other side's total and at least 3 more; otherwise **Marginal**. Equal totals: a draw (both Marginal).
<!-- /table -->

**Raids** are graded by the mission type (above); casualty points don't count.

## Example

A patrol game, **Raid: seize data or cargo**, aboard a freighter (chapter 11). The roll-off goes to the Hankou player, who attacks with SPEAR 1 (Team Leader, Automatic Rifleman, Pioneer, Rifleman: 276 TV). The Anchorage player defends with 271 TV: a chalk (Scout, Scout, Grenadier: 192 TV) and the Prime (79).

- **The split.** The guard can be at most a quarter of 271 (67 TV): one Scout (66) at the data terminal on the bridge. The reserve must be at least a quarter: the Prime (79), off the board. The garrison is the other Scout and the Grenadier, in one blind, with three decoys.
- **Deployment.** The defender places the four blinds more than 12" from the airlock and outside the docking passage. Hankou deploys in the docking passage and activates first.
- **Play.** The Pioneer's first shot is Loud: the alert track goes to 1, and the Scout on the bridge, 10" away aboard ship, hears it. The alarm is raised at the end of that activation, taking the track to 2, and the garrison's blinds start moving. A check-in at minute 10 takes it to 3. When the Rifleman blows the bridge door with a breaching charge (Very loud), it reaches 5: the Prime arrives at the far end of the spine at the end of the round.
- **The grade.** If Hankou gets the data out before the clock runs out with no one Dead, it is Decisive for Hankou and a Failure for Anchorage.
