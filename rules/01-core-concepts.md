# 1. Core concepts

## What you need

- **Models:** one per soldier, on 25–32 mm bases.
- **Dice:** two d6 in one colour (the **hit dice**), and a pair each of d4, d6, d8, d10 and d12 in a second colour (the **damage dice**).
- **Tokens:** see the table below.
- **Terrain:** for boarding actions, 6"×6", 6"×3" and 3"×3" tiles with walls and doors.
- **A ruler** measuring in inches.

### Tokens

Any tokens will do. The last column is a suggested set using six colours of small cubes.

| Token | Where | Meaning | Cube |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Activated** | Beside the model | It has activated, or reacted as an unactivated model, this round. Remove all at the end of the round. | Blue |
| **Overwatch** | Beside the model | Place two when it goes on Overwatch; remove one for each reaction, and any left when it next activates. | Green |
| **Suppression** | Beside the model | One per point, at most 5. | Yellow |
| **Wounded** | Beside the model | One per Wounded marker. | Red |
| **Down** | — | Lay the model on its side. | — |
| **Bleeding / Critical** | On a Down model | Rounds left to live: 3 for Bleeding, 1 for Critical. Remove one at the end of each later round; when the last is removed, it dies. | Black |
| **Surrendered** | On the model | Lay it down and mark it. | White |
| **Last-known position** | On the floor | The enemy's marker (chapter 10). | White |
| **Locked** | On the door | The door is locked. | Black |
| **Hack progress** | On the door or console | Red for each failed attempt (+1 each, up to 3); white for each success. | Red, white |
| **Breaching charge** | On the door or wall | A placed charge. | Any spare token |
| **Contacts** | On the floor | Unrevealed enemies (missions). | Numbered chips |
| **Fire, smoke** | On a 3" cell | Hazards (chapters 6 and 8). | Any marker, or cotton wool for smoke |

An enemy element that is **Alerted**: turn its unit cards sideways.

A typical game uses about 20 yellow, 12 red, 12 blue, 12 green, 8 black and 6 white.

**Scale:** 1" = 2 m. A 6" tile is a 12 m compartment; a 3"-wide corridor is 6 m across.

## Model profile

Every model has a **rank**, a set of **attributes**, **Armour**, its weapons and gear, and **skills**.

- **Rank** (Green, Regular, Veteran, Elite) is a label for experience and standing. It never enters a roll; the attributes do. Campaign veterancy raises individual attributes and skills, and promotes the rank.
- **Attributes** say what the model is good at. Each rule names the attribute it uses.
- **Armour** (0–3) steps wound results down (chapter 4).
- **Skills** are traits such as *Leader*, *Medic* or *Void-trained*. Some have levels, written *Marksman 2*; a higher level includes everything below it.

Attributes usually run 0–3, and 4 is exceptional; −1 is possible for the truly untrained. On 2d6, each point is worth roughly 14 percentage points at the usual target of 8. A dash means the attribute is never used (*Automated* models).

## Tests

A **test** is 2d6 plus the attribute the rule names, minus 1 per Suppression marker and minus 1 if the model has any Wounded markers, against a target number. The target is **8** unless a rule says otherwise. A natural 2 always fails. Rules write this as, for example, "test 2d6 + Nerve, 8+".

## Measuring

Measure in inches, base edge to base edge. You may measure at any time.

## Line of sight

Use true line of sight from the model's eye. A model can see a target if any part of the target's body (not weapon or antenna) is visible. Walls and closed doors block line of sight. Other models do not block it, but they give the target Light cover.

## Rounding and modifiers

Modifiers are cumulative. Round fractions of inches down. When a rule says "half", round down.

## Order of precedence

When a specific rule conflicts with a general one, the specific rule wins. When two rules conflict and neither is more specific, the one less favourable to the activating player applies.
